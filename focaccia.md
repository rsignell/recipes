# Chef Billy Parisi's Focaccia alla Genovese (modified)

A traditional Ligurian Focaccia alla Genovese utilizing a multi-day biga pre-ferment, a balanced ~75% hydration dough, and a warm water-and-olive-oil emulsion (salamoia) poured into deep finger dimples before baking on a low oven rack.

* Yield: 1 sheet pan (13x18 inch half-sheet or 12x16 inch pan)
* Prep time: 20 mins (+ 8–24 hrs biga rise + 2 hrs dough proof)
* Convection Bake time: 18–22 mins @ 425°F (oven preheated to 450°F)

#### Ingredients

**Biga (pre-ferment, Day 1 evening)**
* Bread flour: 335 g
* Water (room temp, ~70°F): 160 g
* Active dry yeast: 1 g (approx. ⅓ tsp)

**Main dough (Day 2)**
* All of the biga (from Day 1)
* Bread flour: 335 g
* Warm water (100°F–105°F): 340 g total (split into 255 g and 85 g)
* Active dry yeast: 3 g (1 tsp)
* Fine sea salt: 10 g
* Malt syrup: 5 g
* Extra-virgin olive oil: 25 g

**Pan, brine & topping**
* Extra-virgin olive oil for pan: 30 g (2 tbsp)
* Extra-virgin olive oil for emulsion: 30 g (2 tbsp)
* Warm water (brine): 80 g
* Flaky Maldon sea salt for finishing: 10 g
* (optional) Fresh rosemary: 2–3 sprigs, leaves picked and roughly chopped, massaged in oil

#### Instructions

1. Build the biga (Day 1, evening): In a small bowl, combine 335 g bread flour, 160 g room-temperature water, and 1 g active dry yeast. Mix with a spoon or your hands until a firm, shaggy dough ball forms. Cover tightly with plastic wrap and let ferment at room temperature (70°F–72°F) for 8–24 hours.
2. Mix main dough (Day 2): Tear the fermented biga into smaller pieces and drop them into the bowl of a stand mixer fitted with the dough hook. Add 335 g bread flour and 255 g warm water, then autolyze for 20 minutes. Add the remaining 85 g warm water, 3 g active dry yeast, 10 g fine sea salt, 5 g malt syrup, and 25 g extra-virgin olive oil.
3. Knead in stand mixer: Knead on low speed for 2 minutes to combine, then increase to medium (speed 4 on a KitchenAid) and knead for 8 to 10 minutes. The dough should completely pull away from the sides of the bowl, clinging only slightly to the bottom.
4. Pan proof & initial stretch: Pour 30 g (2 tbsp) of extra-virgin olive oil into your pan, coating the bottom while leaving a 1-inch zone free around the edge so the dough can more easily stretch to the corners. Transfer the dough to the pan. Cover and rest for 15 minutes to relax the gluten, then push it fully into the corners. Cover and let rise for 60–90 minutes until light and puffy.
5. Build the brine emulsion: Set your oven rack to the bottom-third position and preheat the oven to 450°F. In a small measuring cup or bowl, add the warm water (80 g), and while whisking continuously (or using an immersion blender), stream in 30 g (2 tbsp) of extra-virgin olive oil until a pale, cloudy emulsion forms.
6. Brine & dimple: Pour the emulsified brine evenly over the surface of the puffed dough. Coat your hands in olive oil and press your fingers straight down through the liquid until you touch the bottom of the pan to create deep, wide dimples across the entire surface. Optionally sprinkle with chopped fresh rosemary leaves and flaky sea salt.
7. Bake: Convection bake at 425°F on the bottom-third rack for 18–22 minutes until the top is deeply golden brown, the dimples remain soft and pale, and the bottom crust is crisp and sizzling in olive oil.
8. Cool: Transfer the focaccia immediately from the pan to a wire cooling rack to prevent the bottom crust from steaming and softening. Wait 10 minutes before slicing.

#### Notes & Tips

* Hydration target: This formula yields a ~75% hydration dough (74.6%) based on 670 g total flour (335 g biga + 335 g main dough) and 500 g total water (160 g biga + 340 g main dough).
* Emulsion technique: Stream the olive oil slowly into the warm water while whisking to build a stable emulsion (salamoia) that coats the top and pools gently in the dimples without instantly splitting.
* Rack placement: Placing the pan on the bottom-third rack ensures the olive oil shallow-fries the bottom crust to a crisp golden finish while protecting the top toppings from burning.
